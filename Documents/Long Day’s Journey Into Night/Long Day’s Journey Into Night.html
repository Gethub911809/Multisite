Long Day’s Journey Into Night

Author

Aidan Freeman

Happy to say this is my first venture into Bi Gan’s filmography.

Long Days Journey into Night moves like no other movie. The constant use of mirrors, reflections, and odd angles make for such a unique experience. It draws from classic monumental works in slow cinema like work from Bela Tárr or Tarkovsky, but never feels derivative.

The beginning is shot extremely meticulously. Almost Wes Anderson in nature sometimes with how houses are portrayed sideways and with extreme color. Bi Gan makes every frame gorgeous here and that aids his slow style greatly. Without the beautiful and interesting angles and shots the slow approach would be much less interesting and the atmosphere would not be melancholic and contemplative, but rather bland and dull. It takes a special eye to pull off slower paced film and Bi Gan has it.

The 2nd half is shot more down to earth with a sweeping one take. It is handheld following a character, which grounds us in the dream state. It’s funny how he uses the grounded camera approach in order to portray a dream, but a more artistic approach when portraying reality.

Thematically this is a beast as well. Longing explored in cinema is best explored through slower cinema. The length draws out our emotions and makes us feel what he does. Longing is something I have personally felt, and this perfectly replicates that feeling. It does this better than any film in recent memory.

Similarly to Elephant Sitting Still, the visual metaphors are explained, and this film isn’t one to be analyzed for themes or ideas. It is a film of feeling. I often hate the phrase, “Just feel it,” championed by those wanting to defend movies without integrity, but this phrase is definitely applicable here. A mood so strong and visuals so striking moving and breathing with the film feels the only thing natural. Moves along to the tempo of your soul and envelops you in a crisp cooked torilla, in which you are the contents, including but not limited to, meat, cheese, chilis, and guac.

The structure of this is just so phenomenal.
One of the greater films of the century.
